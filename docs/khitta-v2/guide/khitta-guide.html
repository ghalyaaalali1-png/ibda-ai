01 · The big picture

02 · The founder's journey

How KHITTA works with a founder, step by step

  1. Describe the idea. One or two sentences: what they'll sell, where, and the budget — e.g. "a home bakery in Dubai, selling online, budget AED 15,000".
  2. At most two questions. KHITTA reads the sentence and only asks what is missing (location, budget or sales channel). If nothing is missing, it goes straight to the summary.
  3. Confirm the summary. The founder checks the details, changes anything, and starts the analysis. Signed in, the business is saved as a project first.
  4. The analysis. The Agent Workspace shows each stage while Claude prepares the plan — usually about a minute.
  5. The plan. Verdict and score, budget and costs, price and margins, break-even, market, risks, next actions and UAE licensing steps.
  6. Suppliers, Marketing, Launch Pack. Supplier types with quote requests; a 13-week marketing plan with a launch-month calendar; one consolidated launch checklist.
  7. Keep, edit, share. The project lives in My projects; Edit details updates only what changed (with history); Download PDF saves the whole plan.
  8. Any time, either language. EN / عربي switches the whole interface and the plan's content; figures stay the same.
Without an account the full journey still works in the browser; the plan just isn't saved. After signing in, KHITTA offers to save it to My projects — with no new AI work.

03 · Inside the agent

How one plan is made

Every plan is built from eight sections ("modules"). Each is one request to Claude (model claude-opus-5-5) with its own instructions and a strict output shape:

1

Assessment

Idea summary, target customer, positioning, feasibility verdict, assumptions, open questions.

2

Financials

Budget, startup costs, prices, unit costs, monthly sales, market price bands.

3

Narrative

Market, risks, ordered next actions, what to source, marketing focus.

4

Setup

UAE licence routes, steps, requirements — only from verified official sources.

5

Suppliers

Supplier types, first orders and quote questions — sized to the plan's sourcing budget.

6–8

Marketing (3 parts)

Strategy and 13-week plan; content, budget split and KPIs; launch-month posts and competitor patterns.

The order

Seven sections run at the same time. Suppliers waits for Financials, so first orders fit the money the plan already set aside for stock. Each answer is checked for empty parts and retried once if needed.

The rules the agent follows

  • Accuracy over completeness. Never invent facts, brands, suppliers, prices, fees, licence names or statistics.
  • Every important figure is labelled: your figure, estimate, or to be confirmed.
  • Licences, fees and eligibility are never stated as fact — the founder is pointed to the official authority.
  • No guarantees of success; planning guidance, not financial or legal advice.
Written into the code, not left to the AI: Claude can only cite official sources from KHITTA's verified list, for the founder's emirate. Anything else is dropped, and the UAE government portal (u.ae) is shown instead.

04 · Numbers and the verdict

Claude estimates the inputs; KHITTA does the maths

Every total on screen is calculated by the app from Claude's estimates, so the numbers always add up.

FigureHow it's worked out
Startup costsClaude's cost lines; if they exceed the budget they are scaled to 90% of it, and the rest is kept as a cash reserve.
Gross margin(Price − product costs) ÷ price.
Contribution per salePrice − all costs per sale (product, payment, delivery).
Cash over 6 monthsStarts at minus the startup spend; each month adds sales × contribution, minus running costs.
Break-evenThe first month the cumulative cash turns positive — or "beyond month 6".
Marketing budgetThe plan's marketing cost lines, split by Claude's suggested shares.

The verdict

GO

The idea, budget and plan look workable on what's known.

CAUTION

Workable with specific changes, listed as conditions.

NO-GO

A significant issue should be resolved first.

Launch readiness (Launch Pack)

Rules over what's already been generated — never a new judgement: Ready to launch, Proceed with conditions (e.g. no break-even in 6 months, requirements to confirm, open questions), or Needs work (e.g. NO-GO, or the price doesn't cover costs).

Why this matters: the founder can see why the plan says what it says. If the verdict and the numbers disagree, KHITTA shows the disagreement rather than hiding it.

05 · Two languages

Arabic and English, switchable at any time

The interface

Every label and message lives in two dictionaries (English and Arabic) that are tested to match. Arabic is right-to-left throughout, with IBM Plex Sans Arabic.

The plan's content

A new plan is written by Claude in the language the founder is using. Codes and IDs stay in English inside the data.

Switching language later

The saved plan is translated once and the translation is kept, so switching back and forth is instant afterwards.

Same figures, always

Only text is translated. Numbers, codes and structure are checked to be identical before a translation is saved.

What stays as written

  • Answers are stored in English codes (e.g. "Online only"), so the plan's input never depends on the interface language.
  • Brand and platform names (Instagram, WhatsApp), official authority names and website addresses.
  • A business name typed in one language is translated for the other and saved with the project.
Tested: a real Arabic plan, the Arabic demo and translated plans are checked automatically for any English left on screen, and for identical figures.

09 · Security and privacy

Rules written into the code

These aren't policies on paper — each is enforced by the database or the server, whatever the AI says.

RuleHow it's enforced
Each user sees only their own dataRow Level Security on every table; a project link opened by anyone else shows "not found". Tested with 33 automated checks.
History can't be rewrittenSaved versions of answers and plan sections can only be added, never changed.
Visitors without an accountGet no access to any table.
Keys never reach the browserThe Claude key and secrets live only in server settings (.env.local, never committed).
AI cost protectionHourly limits per person: 6 plans without an account, 10 plans or edits with one, 30 translations, 40 captions.
Safe sign-in linksAfter signing in, KHITTA only ever redirects inside KHITTA.
Honest guidanceOnly verified official sources; figures labelled as estimates; disclaimers on every plan and PDF.
Up-to-date librariesSecurity audit: 0 known vulnerabilities at the time of writing.

11 · Running and costs

What you need to run KHITTA

Run it on your Mac

  1. Settings file. .env.local holds three values: ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL and NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY. It is never committed.
  2. Database. Run the three files in supabase/migrations, in order, in Supabase's SQL Editor (already done for the current project).
  3. Start. npm install, then npm run dev, and open localhost:3000.
  4. Check. npm test, npm run lint and npm run build should all pass before any change is saved.

Running costs

12 · Before launch

The checklist to go live

Everything needed to move KHITTA from your Mac to real users:

  • Reserve the domain: khitta.ae through an aeDA-accredited registrar.
  • Hosting: deploy the app; add the three settings in the host's secret settings (never in code).
  • Supabase addresses: add the real web address to Site URL and Redirect URLs.
  • Google sign-in: add the real address in Google Cloud, then publish the app (it is "Testing" today).
  • Email sending: connect a sending service (custom SMTP) and paste the branded templates from supabase/email-templates.
  • Legal pages: privacy policy and terms of use, in Arabic and English.
  • Spending alerts: on the Anthropic and Supabase accounts.
  • Backups: confirm Supabase's backup schedule for the plan you choose.
  • Final test: a full journey in both languages, on phone and desktop, on the live address.
Start with the domain and hosting, then email, then Google. Everything else follows.
